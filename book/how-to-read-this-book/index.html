What this book is

This is a complete and evolving textbook on modern AI systems. It takes you from mathematical foundations like vectors, derivatives, probability, all the way to how frontier systems like GPT-class, Claude-class, and leading open source models are built, trained, served, and understood. The main prerequisite is some understanding of, or dogged willingness to learn, basics of calculus and linear algebra.

This book follows certain design principles. It builds every concept from parts discussed in earlier chapters. It expands abbreviations the first time they appear and identifies mathematical symbols. And it generally explains everything in three ways: first in plain English about what is really happening; then in math with derivations and results; and finally in practice, including how frontier models work (which often differs from the textbook version).

And claims connect to sources. Many important ideas came from papers, so we link the papers where ideas appear, and you can view them side-by-side with this text.

💡Intuition

A useful way to hold the whole book in your head: modern AI is one idea compounded. A model is a giant differentiable function; we measure how wrong it is with a loss; calculus tells us which direction to nudge every parameter to be slightly less wrong; we repeat that trillions of times on trillions of words. Everything else — attention, transformers, RLHF, mixture-of-experts — is engineering to make that loop expressive, stable, and efficient at unbelievable scale. When you feel lost in any chapter, come back to this paragraph.

Search everything. Press / (or the magnifying glass) to search the full text of the book.

The book updates. AI moves monthly. Sections marked with a red Update bar are dated addenda; the updates page lists what changed and when. The book’s source lives in a git repository, so adding a new section when something important happens is a one-file change.

The three-layer format, demonstrated

Throughout the book you will see recurring boxes. Here is what each means, demonstrated on an example — the softmax function, which you will meet properly in Chapter 6.

💡Intuition

Softmax turns any list of numbers (“scores”) into a valid probability distribution. Every entry is positive, and they all sum to 1. Bigger scores get disproportionately more of the mass. Softmax is the standard way a model converts raw preferences into probabilities it can sample from - meaning that the model can randomly choose an output according to those probabilities. If softmax gives cat = 70%, dog = 20%, car = 10%, sampling means “pick one, with cat having a 70% chance of being picked.”

⚙️In practice

Every large language model ends in exactly this operation: the network produces a score (a “logit”) for every token in its vocabulary, and softmax converts them into next-token probabilities. The temperature slider you see in every AI playground is the TTT above, applied at sampling time.

The road ahead

The book has seven main parts, plus a reference section (glossary and notation). They are designed to be read in order — later parts lean on earlier ones — but each chapter opens with plain-English framing, so skimming ahead works too.

  • Part I — Mathematical Foundations. Vectors, matrices, high-dimensional geometry, derivatives and the chain rule, probability, information theory. Every tool is introduced with the deep learning job it will do later.
  • Part II — Deep Learning Foundations. Neurons, layers, losses, gradient descent, backpropagation, optimizers, initialization, regularization, normalization, residual connections. This is the anatomy of trainable networks, with worked numerical examples.
  • Part III — Language Modeling. What it means to model language probabilistically, tokenization, embeddings, and how language models are evaluated.
  • Part IV — Attention & Transformers. Attention from first principles, the transformer architecture piece by piece, positional encodings and RoPE, the KV cache, efficient attention, mixture-of-experts, state-space models.
  • Part V — Training LLMs in Practice. Data pipelines, scaling laws, GPU clusters and parallelism, the training run, RLHF and DPO, reasoning training, fine-tuning, inference and serving.
  • Part VI — The Modern AI Landscape. The frontier as it exists in 2026: the major model families, multimodal models, diffusion, long context, agents and tool use, retrieval, evaluation, open vs closed.
  • Part VII — Interpretability. The science of looking inside AI models: features and superposition, sparse autoencoders, circuits, causal methods, steering, chain-of-thought faithfulness, and what it means for safety.
✎Note

Where the field has open debates (does “emergence” exist? do sparse autoencoders find the model’s real features?), this book generally presents the debate rather than picking a winner. Where numbers are fast-moving (benchmark scores, model sizes, prices), it flags them as snapshots. Anything labeled Update was added after the original book was written, though parts may also be updated without noting so.

Ready? Two ways in. For the entire machine at a glance — every part of a frontier model, core definitions and formulas only, one sitting — read The Whole Machine first; the rest of the book then deepens each piece. Or start with vectors, which explains the foundational idea that meaning lives in directions of high-dimensional space.
